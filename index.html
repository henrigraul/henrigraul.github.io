Work in progress

Uninsured Creative Destruction

with Matteo Samarani

Abstract

We propose a theory of creative destruction under incomplete markets. At its core is the observation that creative destruction shapes workers’ labor income process. When the workers exposed to it are the economy’s savers, the risk it creates moves the demand for assets and, with it, the incentives to innovate. In a quality-ladder model, households self-insure against displacement, and their saving affects the return at which entrants are financed. Uninsured displacement risk lowers the return firms must pay and, through free entry, speeds up creative destruction relative to complete markets. Further, the pace of creative destruction rises with consumption inequality between displaced and employed workers, since a wider gap implies a stronger precautionary motive. Finally, we show that welfare in this economy features an equity-growth trade-off that arises from insurance itself: the risk that insurance removes is the saving motive that finances innovation.
